Methodology. We compare prefill attention service costs using the original AttAcc A100a GPU model and bank-level PIM timing model for LLAMA-7B at batch one. Q new queries attend to C resident cached tokens plus Q new tokens. MQ groups up to eight queries per scan while keeping query-private movement and softmax explicit. Q input precedes PIM execution. New-KV transfer overlaps only the first query group's old-cache scan before its first new-K access; the uncovered part is charged. GPU execution includes historical-KV readback because the cache initially resides on PIM. The comparison covers per-layer attention, including the specified transfers, rather than end-to-end request latency.

Evaluation. At C=1024 and a 300-GB/s link, ordinary PIM gives way to GPU at integer Q=55. MQ extends PIM's favorable region to roughly 512 queries, followed by several small reversals over Q=512--545 due to query-group tails and discrete scan timing. We therefore report a transition region instead of enforcing a single monotonic threshold. At Q=200, MQ service latency is 109.311 microseconds versus 157.794 microseconds for GPU, a 30.73% reduction. This saving combines lower scan/operator cost and less exposed transfer. Cold prefills show no PIM advantage over the sampled Q range. These observations depend on cache residency and the original unfused GPU model, not just arithmetic intensity.
