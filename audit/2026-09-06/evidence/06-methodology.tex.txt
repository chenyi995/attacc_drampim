\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
\label{sec:integration}

\textbf{Platform and simulator.}
\label{sec:method-attacc}
The platform is DGX-AttAcc~\cite{attacc}, eight A100 GPUs
(the HBM3 variant AttAcc models) each with a PIM-enabled
HBM3 stack over NVLink, the GPUs
holding the weights and running the linear layers and the
stacks holding the KV.
The evaluation runs on kvpim-sim, an event-stream simulator
on Ramulator2~\cite{ramulator2,ramulator21} and
AttAcc~\cite{attacc}.
Its analytic mode costs each operator and times the bank
scans on Ramulator2 for the placement ladder.
Its physical event-DAG mode expands each request layer by
layer for the mechanism checks.
\Fugue{} leaves the DRAM command set and the GPU attention
kernel unchanged, keeps the MAC as the bank PE's only
arithmetic, and adds the logic-die units of
Section~\ref{sec:die}.
The bank efficiency $\eta$, the FFN, and the GPU--PIM
pipelining follow AttAcc's methodology, and energy is per
command from its HBM3 model~\cite{attacc,hbmpim}.

\textbf{RTL implementation.}
Matched AttAcc and \Fugue{} stacks were implemented in RTL at
the bank, the bank group (BG), the logic die, and the HBM
controller: $1024$ bank
GEMV units, $256$ bank-group accumulators with their buffers,
one logic die of $16$ channels, and one HBM
controller~\cite{attacc}.
The storage of the DRAM arrays and
the die budget it sits in are constants of the HBM3
part~\cite{hbm3jssc,papi}, accounted separately.

\label{sec:method-syn}
All designs are synthesized with Cadence Genus against the
ASAP7 $7$\,nm library~\cite{asap7}, AttAcc's bank GEMV units at its
$666$\,MHz and \Fugue{}'s at the $1.30$\,GHz balanced
frequency point of Section~\ref{sec:eval-balance}, and the
softmax array whole at $1.30$\,GHz for both.
The bank and bank-group areas are reported at $10\times$ the
ASAP7 number, the DRAM density penalty AttAcc
cites~\cite{attacc}, while the logic die and the controller
sit on the buffer die and are reported as synthesized.
The energy reported below is the per-command energy of the
HBM3 model~\cite{attacc,ramulator2}.

\textbf{Comparison points.}
\label{sec:method-ladder}
Seven points form a ladder in which each step changes one
thing, named as in the simulator.
A1 is AttAcc~\cite{attacc}, the no-reuse reference, one dense
KV copy per request with decode in the banks and every
prefill on the GPU.
A2 adds software sharing and runs every attention on the
GPU, with the shared KV held in a store used as memory
only.
A3b moves decode into the banks with the shared KV resident
there: a head's chunks and its repairs are appended in one
stream, rotated over the channels that head owns, so a
repair sits in a row of its own and the repairs of
different rounds are separated by whatever was written
between them.
A4c adds the per-agent diff of
Section~\ref{sec:provision-ratio}: a head's repairs are
packed into diff rows on one of that head's channels, while
the master rows keep every channel the head owns.
A4e adds the placement table of Section~\ref{sec:table} for
the master rows, decided when a chunk is written, in place
of the write-order rotation.
A5 places every prefill attention in the banks and drives
the shared rows by the MQ-MAC command
(Section~\ref{sec:mq}).
A6 is \Fugue{}, adding the per-prefill event-based placement
model.

\textbf{Workloads.}
\label{sec:method-agentic}
\label{sec:method-workloads}
A workload is a tiered directed acyclic graph of agents, one
node per request, whose tiers are joined by the dataflow
patterns a multi-agent framework is built from, fan-out,
fan-in, all-to-all, and pipeline.
A generator turns four integers into such a graph, the width
$N$ of a tier, the shared corpus size $C_{\mathrm{blk}}$ in
$256$-token blocks, the depth $D$, and the number $k$ of
tokens each agent recomputes in a shifted block.
Every agent carries its own earlier outputs as history, and
the shared blocks enter at positions of their own, so a
block is read at a different position by each agent that
reads it.
Fourteen configurations sweep those axes around an
all-to-all baseline of $N{=}16$, $C_{\mathrm{blk}}{=}32$,
$D{=}2$, and
$k{=}8$, one axis at a time to $N\in\{4,64\}$,
$C_{\mathrm{blk}}\in\{16,64\}$, $D\in\{1,4\}$, and
$k\in\{2,32\}$, together with the broadcast, reduce,
supervisor, pipeline, and private-corpus topologies.
The configurations set the agents and the tokens rather than
the model, so the same fourteen serve every model and every
rung.

\textbf{Models.}
\label{sec:method-models}
Six models run in three system configurations, LLaMA-7B and
LLaMA3-8B on one GPU with one stack, GPT-13B and LLaMA-33B
on two GPUs with ten, and GPT-175B and LLaMA-65B on eight
GPUs with forty~\cite{llama,llama3,gpt3}.
The configuration fixes how many KV heads share a stack's
sixteen channels, and so how many channels a head owns,
which is what the diff-row and placement-table rungs act on.
LLaMA3-8B is the grouped-query member, four query heads to
one KV head, and the rest are multi-head, all served with
RoPE in BF16.
Six models over fourteen configurations and seven rungs give
$588$ runs.

\textbf{Real workloads.}
\label{sec:method-traces}
\label{sec:method-policies}
The real-workload experiment uses three public datasets,
converted to the simulator's request schema in which equal
segment fingerprints across requests mark shareable KV: a
Mooncake~\cite{mooncake} production tool-and-agent trace,
ShareGPT~\cite{sharegpt} multi-turn conversations, and
MultiHop-RAG~\cite{multihoprag} queries over shared
documents, all under multi-turn serving.
The ladder uses the EPIC~\cite{epic} policy at $k{=}8$, and
at \Fugue{} the policy is swept over
CacheBlend~\cite{cacheblend}, Cache-Craft~\cite{cachecraft},
and CacheTune~\cite{cachetune} so the placement result is
read against the software layer rather than at one policy.
\textbf{Metrics.}
The reported metrics are the time to first token per
prefill, the per-token decode latency weighted by the steps
each tier takes, the KV bytes against the no-reuse layout,
the fraction of prefills the rule sends to the banks, and
the area of the additions.
The physical event engine, which expands each request layer
by layer, is the mode every reported number comes from.
The analytic mode serves as a coarse check on it.
